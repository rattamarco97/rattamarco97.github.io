I am a **Statistical Methodologist at Saryga** and an early-career researcher in statistics. I obtained my PhD in Statistics from **Politecnico di Torino**, where my doctoral research focused on the development of statistical methodology for clinical trials.

My research combines methodological and applied statistical work, with the aim of developing efficient and practically relevant approaches for pharmaceutical development and clinical research. I am particularly interested in Bayesian methods, adaptive designs, and the use of multiple sources of evidence to improve decision-making in clinical trials.

## Research Interests

My main research interests include:

- **Bayesian Statistics and Historical Borrowing**  
  Development of Bayesian methods for incorporating historical and external information into clinical trials, with particular interest in robust mixture priors, dynamic borrowing, and prior-data conflict.

- **Adaptive and Group Sequential Clinical Trials**  
  Statistical methodology for interim monitoring, treatment selection, sample size reassessment, and adaptive decision-making.

- **Seamless Phase II/III Designs**  
  Development of efficient methods for treatment and dose selection while maintaining rigorous confirmatory inference.

- **Benefit–Risk Assessment and Decision-Making**  
  Statistical approaches for combining evidence across multiple clinical outcomes and supporting decision-making throughout drug development.

- **Surrogate Endpoints and Time-to-Event Methods**  
  Methodology for the use of surrogate information and efficient inference in survival and other time-to-event clinical trial settings.

## Background

I obtained my PhD in Statistics from the **Politecnico di Torino** in April 2026. My PhD was funded by **Servier** and supervised by Prof. Mauro Gasparini, with collaborations involving researchers from both academia and industry.

During my doctoral studies, I worked on several methodological problems arising in clinical trial design and pharmaceutical development, including Bayesian dynamic borrowing, group sequential designs, historical information, surrogate endpoints, and benefit–risk driven treatment selection.

Since November 2025, I have been working as a **Statistical Methodologist at Saryga**, while continuing my academic research activities.

## Research Output

My work has been published in journals including *Statistics in Medicine*, *Pharmaceutical Statistics*, and *PNAS Nexus*. My current research focuses on the development of new statistical methodology for adaptive and Bayesian clinical trials.

A complete list of my publications is available on the [Publications](/publications/) page. You can also find my work through [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=rPkTPigAAAAJ&hl=it) and [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0007-0614-5656).

## Current Interests

I am currently particularly interested in:

- Bayesian dynamic borrowing and hybrid clinical trials;
- adaptive and seamless clinical trial designs;
- treatment and dose selection;
- multi-endpoint and benefit–risk decision-making;
- efficient statistical inference after data-driven selection;
- the integration of internal and external sources of evidence.

I am always interested in discussing potential research collaborations and methodological problems related to clinical trial design and statistical decision-making.
